# BASE
current on chain condition of base
As of mid-February 2026, Base — Coinbase's Ethereum Layer-2 (L2) blockchain built on the OP Stack — stands as one of the most dominant and mature Ethereum scaling solutions. Launched in 2023, Base has evolved from an experimental consumer-focused chain into the leading L2 by multiple key metrics, capturing a significant share of on-chain activity, revenue, and user adoption amid broader L2 consolidation.
Key On-Chain Metrics and Network Health
Base maintains strong fundamentals despite some short-term fluctuations observed in early 2026.

Total Value Locked (TVL): Approximately $3.96 billion in DeFi TVL (per DeFiLlama data as of recent updates). This represents roughly 46% of total Ethereum L2 DeFi TVL, down slightly from peaks above $5 billion in late 2025 but still dominant. Stablecoin market cap on Base sits around $4.67 billion, with USDC holding ~90% dominance, underscoring heavy reliance on Coinbase-integrated stablecoin flows.
Activity and Usage: Daily transactions frequently exceed hundreds of thousands, with historical peaks in the millions during high periods. Recent dashboards show active addresses in the range of several hundred thousand per day, supported by organic growth rather than pure incentives. Base processed billions of transactions cumulatively in prior years, outpacing Ethereum mainnet in day-to-day volume.
Fees and Revenue: 24-hour chain fees hover around $160,000, with nearly all fees accruing as revenue to the protocol (minimal sequencer surplus burn or other deductions visible in aggregates). This has positioned Base to capture ~60%+ of total L2 revenue in 2025 trends continuing into 2026, generating tens of millions annually and making it one of the few profitable L2s.
User Base and Distribution Advantage: Base benefits enormously from Coinbase's ~9+ million monthly active users (from recent filings), enabling seamless on-ramping without heavy reliance on airdrops or external liquidity mining. Applications like USDC transfers see tens of thousands of daily filtered active users, while broader adoption spans DeFi (Aerodrome, Morpho lending), payments, and emerging on-chain use cases.

Recent short-term trends show minor TVL dips (e.g., -3% in some weekly snapshots) and revenue variability, but overall growth from early 2025 levels remains substantial. Base's low fees (fractions of a cent) and high throughput continue to attract volume, especially as Ethereum mainnet reinforces its role as a settlement layer post-upgrades like Pectra.
Development Activity and GitHub Insights
Base's core development repositories (under base-org on GitHub) reflect a mature, production-focused project rather than hyper-active early-stage development.

The primary organization (base-org) was archived in February 2025, signaling that core protocol maintenance has stabilized with fewer new commits needed for the base layer itself. Forks and related repos (e.g., chains, bridge implementations) show sporadic updates into 2026, with recent commits as late as early February focused on bridge improvements, wrap token scripts, and cross-chain tooling.
While not among the absolute top chains for raw commit volume (e.g., compared to Ethereum core or high-velocity L1s like Mina/ICP in broader crypto rankings), Base's activity emphasizes ecosystem tooling, integrations, and maintenance over frequent consensus-breaking changes. This aligns with its OP Stack heritage — heavy upstream reliance on Optimism reduces redundant commits while allowing focus on Coinbase-specific enhancements (e.g., fiat on-ramps, stablecoin flows).

Development remains active in satellite projects (bridges to other chains like Solana) and ecosystem contributions, but the archived status indicates confidence in protocol maturity rather than abandonment.
Ecosystem Positioning and Strategic Outlook
Base's dominance stems from unique advantages:

Direct Coinbase integration provides unmatched user acquisition.
Focus on stablecoins, payments, and real-world asset/tokenization aligns with Coinbase's 2026 priorities ("Everything Exchange" vision).
Revenue leadership among L2s supports sustainability without aggressive token incentives.

Challenges include occasional criticism (e.g., Vitalik Buterin noting corporate control implications) and pivots like shelving certain Base App social features in favor of trading focus. Speculation around a potential native BASE token launch persists (possibly mid-to-late 2026), which could tie value accrual more directly to Coinbase but remains unconfirmed.
In summary, Base in February 2026 is a highly successful, consolidated L2 leader: commanding ~46% L2 TVL share, generating outsized revenue, sustaining robust daily activity through organic/enterprise channels, and operating with mature (though less frantic) development. It exemplifies the shift toward a few dominant Ethereum-aligned rollups amid broader L2 power-law dynamics.
